# Review of main, 2026-10-31

Five independent reviewers read main at f240e69 (execution core, memory and terms, builtins, reader/writer/I/O, loading/modules/mosac) and proved each finding with a query they ran. Items already listed in `docs/iso-notes.md` or in the Doubts/Open lines of `docs/STATUS.md` were left out. ✔ marks findings that were reproduced again before this file was written.

What held up: no program out of more than 40 behaved differently under `mosac` than in the interpreter; no missed or doubly visited GC root was found under `MOSA_GC_STRESS=1`; cut transparency, catch/throw, bagof/setof (8.10 Examples), the logical update view (7.5.4), the error order of functor/arg/univ/atom builtins, and the stream errors of 7.10/8.11 behaved as the standard says.

How to use this file: take one item per session (AGENTS.md). Items marked **decision** need an ADR or an "A or B?" question in `docs/iso-notes.md` before any code. When an item is done, change its status to `done (<commit>)`.

### M1. `-9223372036854775808` cannot be read ✔
- Status: done (8114cbb). - Repro: `read` of `-9223372036854775808.` gives `syntax_error(integer_overflow)`; `writeq` of `min_integer` therefore does not read back, and a file with that literal fails under mosa and mosac.

### M3. First-argument indexing scans every clause on each call ✔ (decision)
- Status: done (f207068, 77793fd, 8a2e461, 3409183, c89d927, 98c4b8c, 56cd60d, d971944; ADR 0015). Every clause has an id that never changes or repeats and the generations at which it was added and removed, and a scan sees the clauses alive at its own generation (7.5.4), so asserta and removals renumber nothing. A call or clause/2 or retract with an unbound first argument walks a cursor (next id, generation) instead of listing every clause; with a bound first argument, a predicate of 8 or more clauses takes its candidates from a first-argument index that assertz and asserta extend, and retract, retractall and clause/2 use the same index. A backtrack or redo reads the top choice point in place instead of copying its candidates. A removed clause is erased at once when it is first or last and no open scan can still reach it, otherwise in one sweep once the removals since the last sweep exceed half the clauses; `Impl::scan_log` records only the choice points of predicate calls and of clause/2 (a retract scan is not recorded: it skips clauses another retract removed, so it never reaches one), so a removal does not walk the whole choice stack. Against 37cf642, goal only, ms (docs/bench.md "Cost of the clause store and index"): scan of 100,000 clauses 2,501 to 16, 40,000 assertas 5,584 to 45, lookup by key of 20,000 clauses 1,268 to 18, 10,000 retracts by key 3,246 to 14, 10,000 clause/2 by key 13,871 to 15, 5,000 retracts from the front without a cut 9,159 to 8 (20,000: 458 s to 35 ms); the six bench programs are within 5% (the slowest, `nrev` on the interpreter, is +2.4%). Left: (a) `loop :- retract(todo(X)), work(X), loop. loop.` stays quadratic: each pass leaves the base-clause choice point, so every removal checks an open scan per pass (main was quadratic too; about 3.7 s at 40,000). (b) A retract scan whose remaining matches were all removed by others now ends with its last answer final instead of a choice point that then fails; the answers are the same and 7.5.4 is unaffected. (c) M18 and M19 below. (d) The final review found that a bound first argument still builds the whole bucket per call and that dead front clauses held by an older scan are walked again (M20), and that libc++ allocates a block per predicate (M21). Codex review: a sweep that kept dead clauses for an open scan stopped counting them, so after the scan closed they stayed until another half of the clauses was removed; now they count again once the lowest scan holding them is gone (fixed with clause_scan_test held_sweep).
- Where: `src/machine/index.cpp:40-55`; `src/machine/choice.cpp:220,282` (`const Entry top = machine.stack.back();` copies `candidates`)
- Repro: `(p(_,_), fail ; true)`: 25,000 clauses 174 ms, 50,000 701 ms, 100,000 2,643 ms. Looking up 20,000 clauses by every key: 1,269 ms (5,000: 82 ms). `retract(p(N,_))` 20,000 times: 12 s.
- Expected: SPEC section 6 promises first-argument indexing; time should be linear.
- Decision: index structure and its update on assert/retract — ADR.
