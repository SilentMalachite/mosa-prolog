## Context

A call compares its first argument with every clause and lists every live clause as a candidate, retract and clause/2 unify every clause, asserta shifts the clause vector and every open scan, and each removal erases from the vector and renumbers every open scan, so on N clauses each of these costs O(N) (review 2026-10-31 M3: 20,000 retracts by key 12.9 s, 40,000 assertas 5.6 s, 20,000 `p(_,_), !` 1.1 s).
Every backtrack and clause redo copies the top `Entry` with its candidate list, so one scan over N clauses costs O(N^2).

## Decision

Clauses live in a deque in clause order; each has an id that never changes or repeats (asserta takes one below the first, assertz one above the last) and the database generations at which it was added and removed, and a scan sees exactly the clauses alive at its own generation (the logical update view of 7.5.4). A scan with an unbound first argument keeps a cursor (next id, generation); one with a bound first argument lists candidate ids, taken for predicates of 8 or more clauses from a first-argument index (atom, integer, float with -0.0 = 0.0 and NaN = NaN, list, name/arity; plus keyless clauses) that asserta and assertz extend; retract, retractall and clause/2 use the same index.
A removed clause is erased at once when it is first or last and no open scan of the predicate can still reach it (a scan older than the removal that has not passed it), otherwise in one sweep once the dead clauses left in the list exceed half the clauses (dead clauses a sweep kept for an open scan count again once the lowest scan holding them is gone; until then only later removals count, so a long-lived scan does not cause a sweep on every change); ids do not change, so nothing is renumbered. Backtracking and redo read the top `Entry` in place.

## Rejected alternatives

Keeping positions as candidates (ADR 0004/0008) and renumbering on asserta and removal: asserta and middle removals stay O(N) and an unbound call still lists every clause.
Generation numbers without stable ids, or a linked list of clauses: positions still shift on asserta, and a list loses the random access that `enter_clause`, mosac and the native fingerprint use.

## Consequences

Calls, retracts and clause/2 by a bound first argument cost the bucket plus the keyless clauses, an unbound call and asserta cost O(1) amortized, and a scan over N clauses is linear; a redo finds its clause by id (O(1) while the ids from the first clause up to the wanted one are consecutive, else a binary search before that position; ids are never reused, so a gap also appears when the first clause is removed and asserta follows, or the last is removed and assertz follows, not only after a sweep of the middle).
`dead` becomes "has a removal generation"; `held_by_scan` becomes a check by generation and id, `note_asserta` and the clause half of `note_removed` go; `live_count` becomes a counter. The index holds keys from clause heads only, no heap cells, so the GC does not see it. `std::deque` costs a block per predicate: on libc++ 4 KB for the first clause (`sizeof(Clause)` is 152, 26 clauses per block), so 40,000 one-clause predicates reach 201 MB against 31 MB with main's vector (review M21); libstdc++ about 0.5 KB; MSVC allocates one `Clause` per block, so a clause costs one allocation there. Left open (review M3): `loop :- retract(todo(X)), work(X), loop. loop.` stays quadratic (each pass leaves the base-clause choice point, so every removal checks an open scan per pass; ~3.7 s at 40,000, as on main); a retract scan whose remaining matches were all removed by others ends with its last answer final instead of a choice point that then fails (same answers); a bound first argument built its whole bucket list per call and the removed front clauses an older open scan could still reach were walked again from position 0 by every later call and retract (review M20; fixed by the amendment below). Fixed later (review M19): a scan parked by a nested run (a consult directive or an initialization goal inside an open scan) counts as open, so it keeps the removed clauses it can still reach, and a reconsult retires the clauses it replaces in the same way instead of clearing them; a static predicate is not changed by assert or retract, so its first call after the scan closed sweeps them.
Tests: `index_scale` (with 4 times the clauses, each of scan, asserta, unbound first call, lookup by key, retract by key, retractall by key, clause/2 by key, retract scan, clause/2 scan, retract from the front with and without a cut, and update by key takes less than 8 times as long plus 30 ms), `clause_scan_test` and `tests/pl/database.pl` `m3_*`.

## Amendment (2026-11-04, review M20; approved by the owner's request to fix M20)

Context: a call, retract or clause/2 with a bound first argument listed its whole bucket before the first answer (retract and clause/2 also unified every clause of it), so 20,000 calls `q(k,_), !` on 20,000 clauses of one key took 2.53 s and `drain :- retract(q(k,_)), !, drain.` on 10,000 took 7.99 s; and removed front clauses that an older open scan still reaches stay, so under an outer `p(_)` every later unbound call or retract walked them one by one from position 0 (draining 40,000 clauses: 0.89 s against 0.10 s without the outer scan).
Decision: every clause scan (call, retract, clause/2, and retractall's walk) keeps a cursor (next id, generation) and the key it selects on (`Any` for an unbound first argument, `None` for a bound one of no key type, which takes only keyless clauses); a step finds the next candidate at or after the cursor in the clause list or, for 8 or more clauses, in the bucket and the keyless list by a binary search on ids, and keeps a choice point only when a next candidate exists (retract and clause/2 try its head first, as their unbound scans already did); a redo starts from the bucket position the last step left in `Entry::next` when the id there is still the cursor, else by a binary search; a walk stops at the first clause born after the scan began, since it walks only ids at or after a clause the scan already saw, asserta gives an id below every existing id and assertz gives ids and generations that grow together, so every later clause is newer too (adversarial review A1); the "lists candidate ids" sentence of the Decision above no longer applies. A dead clause holds two skips (`skip_all` in the clause list, `skip_key` among the clauses of its own key, keyless clauses counting as one key; 32 bits each, saturating, 1 when it dies): every clause in that id range is dead; a walk that can see no dead clause (retract, or a scan whose generation is at least the predicate's `last_death`) jumps along them and points every clause it jumped from at the end of the jump (path compression); clauses never come back to life, and a consult that fails puts back a copy whose clauses keep the ids, generations and skips they had when copied while `next_id` never shrinks, so no later clause falls inside a restored skip and a skip stays true through asserts, erasures, sweeps and restores (adversarial review A5).
Rejected: one mark past the dead prefix of the clause list (the review's suggestion), since an asserta in front of the dead run and dead clauses of one key between live clauses of another still walk one clause at a time; keeping a position in the bucket instead of an id, since asserta and erasing the front shift bucket positions and a sweep drops the index.
Consequences: a call that stops after k answers costs O(k + log B) on a bucket of B clauses plus the clauses it steps over one by one, and a keyed choice point holds no list (the state limit no longer stops `f(a,_)` choice points on one bucket; `current_predicate/1` still holds a list and is counted); `Clause` grows by 8 bytes to 160 with libc++ (25 per 4 KB deque block, review M21) and `Entry` loses `by_cursor` and gains a 16-byte key (264 bytes). Left open: a retract or clause/2 whose head matches only one of the clauses it walks still tries the rest of them to decide whether to keep a choice point (the determinism of M3), so `retract(q(k, 5))` on one key of N clauses and `retract(q(_, 5))` on N clauses stay O(N), as before (adversarial review A4); a call or clause/2 scan whose predicate lost a clause after the scan began walks the dead clauses it cannot see one at a time, as before. Tests: `index_scale` (keyed call, keyed retract and keyed clause/2 that stop at the first answer, both repros of an older open scan over a dead prefix, keyed and unbound, and open scans redone after many later assertz), `clause_scan_test` (keyed determinism, and a sweep under an open keyed scan that drops the dead clauses of other keys) and `tests/pl/database.pl` `m20_*`.
